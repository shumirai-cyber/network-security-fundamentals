# Activity 7: Common Network Attacks and Threats

## Objective

To identify common network attacks, understand how they work, recognise potential warning signs and learn how to reduce cybersecurity risks.

## 1. Phishing Attacks

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack in which an attacker uses deceptive emails, messages or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information, such as passwords or financial details.

### Warning signs

* Unexpected requests for passwords or personal information.
* Messages that create urgency or threaten account suspension.
* Suspicious links or unexpected attachments.
* Unusual sender addresses or spelling mistakes.

### Prevention

* Verify requests through official communication channels.
* Avoid clicking suspicious links or opening unexpected attachments.
* Use multifactor authentication (MFA).
* Report suspected phishing messages through the appropriate process.

## 2. Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

A man-in-the-middle (MitM) attack occurs when an attacker intercepts communication between two parties and may read or alter the information being exchanged.

### Prevention

* Use trusted networks and secure connections.
* Check that websites use HTTPS and do not ignore certificate warnings.
* Keep devices and applications updated.
* Use properly configured encryption and authentication.

HTTPS helps protect communications, although it does not guarantee that a website itself is trustworthy.

## 3. Denial-of-Service and Distributed Denial-of-Service Attacks

A denial-of-service (DoS) attack attempts to make a system or service unavailable by overwhelming it or exhausting its resources.

A distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack uses multiple sources to overwhelm the target.

### Warning signs

* A website or service becomes unusually slow.
* Large or unusual volumes of incoming traffic.
* Unexpected service interruptions.

These signs can have other causes, so further investigation is needed before confirming an attack.

### Prevention

* Use traffic filtering and rate limiting.
* Monitor network traffic for unusual patterns.
* Use appropriate DDoS protection services.
* Develop an incident response plan.

## 4. IP Spoofing

IP spoofing occurs when an attacker falsifies the source IP address in network packets. This can disguise the apparent origin of traffic or help support other attacks.

### Prevention

* Use network filtering and anti-spoofing controls.
* Monitor unusual network traffic.
* Apply appropriate security rules at network boundaries.
* Investigate suspicious activity using authorised security tools.

## 5. Rogue Wi-Fi and Evil Twin Attacks

A rogue access point is an unauthorised wireless access point connected to or impersonating part of a network. An evil twin attack involves a fraudulent Wi-Fi network designed to imitate a legitimate one.

Attackers may use these networks to deceive users or attempt to intercept communications.

### Prevention

* Verify the correct Wi-Fi network name with the network provider or organisation.
* Avoid connecting to suspicious or unfamiliar networks.
* Do not ignore browser certificate warnings.
* Use secure connections and keep devices updated.
* Avoid entering sensitive information into unexpected login pages.

## 6. Practical Activity: Threat Analysis Scenario

I completed a written threat-analysis exercise.

### Scenario

An employee receives an email claiming that their company account will be suspended unless they click a link and sign in immediately. At the same time, the company's website becomes unusually slow, and the IT team notices a large volume of incoming traffic.

### Findings

**Potential threat 1: Phishing**

The email may be a phishing attempt because it creates urgency and asks the employee to sign in through a link.

**Recommended response:** Avoid the link and verify the request through an official company channel. Report the message according to company procedures.

**Potential threat 2: DoS/DDoS**

The unusually slow website and high volume of incoming traffic could indicate a denial-of-service attack.

**Recommended response:** The IT team should investigate traffic patterns, check service health and use authorised monitoring and mitigation tools.

### Important observation

The scenario contains possible indicators of attacks, but the evidence does not prove that an attack has occurred. Further investigation is required to establish the cause.

## 7. Key Learning Outcomes

After completing this activity, I understand that:

* Phishing attempts to deceive people into disclosing sensitive information.
* MitM attacks can involve intercepting or altering communications.
* DoS and DDoS attacks attempt to disrupt service availability.
* IP spoofing involves falsifying source IP addresses.
* Rogue Wi-Fi and evil twin attacks can expose users to security risks.
* Warning signs should be investigated rather than automatically treated as proof of an attack.
* Security awareness, encryption, monitoring and appropriate network controls help reduce risks.
